The real-world rerun is a candidate-risk corpus with an explicit execution-availability boundary, not a fully labeled benchmark. The sampled human audit turns that rerun into a concrete calibration study rather than a narrative stress test. We sample 96 completed cases across six strata and, at the time of this revision, 76 have been reviewed: 40 are human-reviewed benign and 36 are human-reviewed ambiguous, with no confirmed malicious evidence in the reviewed subset. The audit makes the calibration boundary explicit by showing where real-world candidate-risk alerts are better explained as benign functionality, normal runtime behavior, outward but policy-compatible workflows, or insufficient evidence.
